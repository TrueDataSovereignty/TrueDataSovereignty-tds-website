# What we take from anthropic.com

Brand & web (Fable), 11 November 2026. For Henrik and Kung Barfod.

Henrik's words: "Make anthropics website the main inspiration for ours btw. Base line inspiration. I love their website. The way the logo moves when you scroll, etc. I also like how you read stuff on it. Even though I like what we have right now, but we can make it more immersive."

**The rule:** inspiration only. We take principles, not things. None of their layout, assets, fonts, colours, illustrations or wording is used. Ours stays navy and gold, Lora, and the mycelium.

**How I looked:** I read the home page and one long article in the browser pane on 11 November 2026, at 1280 by 720. I measured type sizes, column widths and spacing from the live page. I could not take pictures of it (the pane was hidden), so how their mark looks while it moves is not observed, only that it sits in a header that stays on screen and is an animated drawing.

## The principles we take

1. **A reading column of about 65 characters.** Their long article runs about 65 characters a line (measured: a 545 px column at about 16.5 px type). Ours: long-form pages get one column of about 60 to 65 characters, never wider.

2. **Room between the lines, and one clear gap between paragraphs.** Their body text has a line height of about 1.5, and one gap of a line and a half between paragraphs. Ours: Lora at 19 px with a line height of 1.65, because Lora needs a little more air than their face.

3. **Few type sizes, far apart.** They use a small label, a body size, and a large statement, with big steps between. Ours: a small spaced label, the body, and the Lora headline. Nothing in between.

4. **Headings sit close to what they introduce.** About twice as much space above a heading as below it (measured: 48 px above, 24 px below). Ours: the same two-to-one rule on reading pages.

5. **One idea per section, and space instead of boxes.** Their sections are few and tall. There are almost no borders, cards or shadows; a flat background and white space do the dividing. Ours: we keep the flat paper and the thin rules, and we do not add boxes.

6. **The mark travels with you.** Their header stays on screen while you scroll, and the mark in it is a small animated drawing, not a still picture. Ours: the header now stays, and our own mark responds to the scroll in our own way (see below).

7. **Motion follows the reader.** What moves is small, and it is tied to the reader's own scrolling. Nothing demands attention. Ours: every new movement is driven by scroll position, and all of it is off when the reader has asked for reduced motion.

8. **Reading pages have a quiet side column.** Their article keeps the facts about the piece (date, category, contents) beside the text, out of its way. Ours: reading pages get a side column with "On this page" and the stage of the product.

## What we built from them, in our identity

- **"Through the layer" mark.** In the header of every page. The branches above the gold line are always there. The gold line starts short and settles to full width in the first part of the scroll. The roots below the line draw in as you read down the page, and are complete at the end. So the mark shows how far you have come *through the layer*. With reduced motion, or without scripts, it is simply the whole logo.
- **A header that stays**, and tightens a little once you leave the top.
- **Passing a line between sections.** Each dark band opens with a full-width gold line that draws across as the band comes into view. It is the ground line from the logo, met again at every change of ground. Sections also rise a few pixels into place as they enter.
- **A long-form reading template** (principles 1 to 4 and 8). Used for the Trust page and the privacy notice, and ready for essays.

## What we did not take

- No warm cream page colour, no clay or orange accent. Ours is paper white, midnight marine and gold.
- No sans headline face and no hand-drawn illustrations. Ours is Lora and the drawn mycelium.
- No card grids of news items, no video, no rounded tiles.
- No copy of their navigation, page order or wording.

## Limits of this study

- One screen size only (1280 by 720). Phone layout not studied.
- Two pages only: the home page and one long article.
- The measurements are from the live page on the day; their site may change.
