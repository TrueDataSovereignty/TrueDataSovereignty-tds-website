How it works

One decision for every request.

The Layer sits on your own machine, between your staff and the AI they use. It reads each request before any model does. Then it sends the request one of three ways.

Which way depends on what is in the request, and on the rules you set.

Fig. 1  The request passes the line. Its names do not.

Route one · Mask

Names leave as placeholders.

The names and numbers it finds are replaced before the request goes to a frontier model. When the answer comes back, the real details are put back, on your side.

Emma Whitfield→⟦PERSON_1⟧
GB33 BUKB 2020 1555 5555 55→⟦IBAN_1⟧
emma.whitfield@example.com→⟦EMAIL_1⟧
+44 7700 900123→⟦PHONE_1⟧

Synthetic example: real router output on made-up text (router a822605). Masking protects who is in the text, not what it says: on this route the model provider still reads the content.

Fig. 2  Below the line, the detail softens, like light through fog.

Route two · Generalise

Telling details leave blurred.

Some details point to one person or one company even without a name: a small town, an exact date, a head count. These are widened before the request leaves, and names are masked as on route one.

Sorsele→northern Sweden
13 June 2027→June 2027
35,897 employees→10,000–50,000 employees

Synthetic example: real router output on made-up text (router a822605). Widened details stay widened in the answer. The model provider still reads the content.

Fig. 3  The thread curls back and seals. Nothing crosses.

Route three · Keep local

Some work goes no further.

The most sensitive work goes to a local model on your own machine or your own server. Nothing is sent to a model provider.

In our example, a note about a confidential deal was too close to the company's confidential list. The Layer kept it.

Synthetic example: a real router decision on made-up text (router a822605). The local model is selected, not yet installed; this route has not run live yet.

You set the rules. The Layer applies them.

For each request, in this order:

  1. It reads the request and finds the names, numbers and telling details in it.
  2. It compares the request with your confidential list.
  3. It picks the route from what it found and from your rules. A missing or unknown setting makes it stricter, never looser.
  4. If The Layer cannot finish its check (any error or timeout), the text stays on your machine. If the AI service itself is down, the request is refused, never sent elsewhere.
  5. It writes a record of the decision, with no text in it. Each record carries the fingerprint of the one before.

What it does not do yet

The limits, stated plainly.

  • Masking protects who is in the text, not what it says.
  • It detects Swedish and Finnish personal ID numbers by checksum today. Norwegian and Danish IDs are built but switched off until they are measured.
  • On fresh wording, the confidential check caught 93 of 200 reworded secrets: 46.5%, 95% range 39.4–53.7%, n = 200, measured on synthetic data, round one (ledger N13). This is our main gap.
  • Joining the turns of one chat can re-identify a person even when each turn passes alone. A fix is built and tested; it is off by default and on for pilots, and its figures are being re-checked.
  • The local model is selected, not yet installed.
  • No ISO 27001 or SOC 2 certification. ISO 27001 is planned.
